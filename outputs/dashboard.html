METHOD

Five pressure rules. One score.

Pressure is a match state, not a reputation and not simply “the death overs”. The rules are observable in real time and auditable ball by ball.

1. Chase pressure2nd innings, after over 4, RRR ≥ 10, ≤ 5 wickets in hand.
2. Death pressureOvers 16–20, within 15 runs of par.
3. Collapse pressure4+ down and behind the game, or 6+ down in the last five overs.
4. Knockout pressureKnockout match, overs 10–20, within 25 runs.
5. Early collapse3+ wickets down in the powerplay and behind the game.
Leverage weightPressure balls are weighted by the expected win-probability swing of the next ball, then by competition tier.
1. STATEScore, wickets, balls, target, RRR, phase, stakes.
2. PRESSUREFive rules plus leverage from the win-probability model.
3. IMPACTRuns above par for batters; runs saved for bowlers.
4. UPLIFTPressure impact minus the player's own non-pressure baseline.
5. PRSOutput 35/40, uplift 20/15, control 20, consistency 15, sample 10.
PRS = 0.35 Output + 0.20 Uplift + 0.20 Control + 0.15 Consistency + 0.10 Sample for batters; bowlers use 0.40 / 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.15 / 0.10. Impact and uplift are shrunk by n/(n+80). Every component is a percentile rank in the eligible pool.

LIMITS

What this model does not know

A selector should use the list as a shortlist, not a verdict.

Limitations

Read before using the ranking

  • No age or date of birth in the pack — no youngest-player analysis.
  • Pressure is observational; we do not measure temperament.
  • No ball-tracking, field placement or catch difficulty.
  • Role and team context are only partly observed.
  • Competition weights are a judgment call (IPL 1.00, T20I 0.90, other T20 0.75).
  • This is a historical ranking, not a forecast.

How to use it

A practical selection memo

Use the top tier as a shortlist. Pair the pressure profile with role fit, availability, recent form and the balance of the XI. The model is strongest when it challenges reputation, not when it replaces scouting.
